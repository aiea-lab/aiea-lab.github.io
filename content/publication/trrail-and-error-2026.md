abstract = "Logical reasoning remains challenging for LLMs, particularly in domains where intermediate reasoning must be inspected and checked. Neuro-Symbolic (NeSy) methods such as SymbCoT, LogicLM, and ProSLM expose structured IRs, but many use static pipelines and are evaluated mainly on logic benchmarks. We investigate multi-agent self-correction in NeSy question answering across law, medicine, and logical reasoning, using Chain-of-Thought (CoT) as a baseline and gpt-5.4-nano and gpt-5.3-chat-latest as backbones. We also introduce the TRRAIL score, an LLM-as-judge rubric for observable reasoning artifacts. TRRAIL abbreviates Transparent, Reasoning-relevant, Reasoning-grounded, Answer-supported, Interpretable, and Logical. Across the tested configurations, refinement benefits the Prolog pipelines, whereas Lean refinement decreases several task and TRRAIL metrics. The results therefore support refinement as a configuration-dependent mechanism rather than a universal improvement. Backbone capability and judge choice also materially affect measured performance and TRRAIL scores, underscoring that artifact verifiability, predictive accuracy, and human trust are distinct questions."
